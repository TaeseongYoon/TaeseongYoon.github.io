Research summary

My research develops uncertainty quantification (UQ) for deep learning that is both efficient and faithful: methods that quantify epistemic uncertainty in a single forward pass while ensuring that the quantified uncertainty genuinely reflects what the model does not know. My work builds on evidential deep learning and efficient Bayesian inference, treating second-order (Dirichlet-based) uncertainty as a Bayesian object and asking what it can and cannot represent. Along this line, I have introduced density-aware (ICML 2024) and flexible (NeurIPS 2025) formulations of second-order uncertainty, an interpretable mixture-of-Dirichlet framework (ICML 2026), and a hierarchical Bayesian approach that separates prediction from epistemic-uncertainty inference. My current work extends this agenda to post-hoc UQ for pretrained and foundation models, and more broadly to UQ for agentic AI systems, where uncertainty must be propagated through multi-step reasoning and tool use. I am also interested in conformal prediction and tabular foundation models as complementary routes to reliable prediction.

The same concern—reliable learning when data are incomplete, imbalanced, or confounded—drives my work on structured and industrial data: multivariate time-series anomaly detection for multi-stage manufacturing processes (IEEE TASE, CIKM 2026), class-imbalanced semi-supervised learning, and variable selection for continuous treatment effect estimation.
